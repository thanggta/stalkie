# Don't Wait Up — authoring note

Case-local. Not a decision record.

## Three pitches

### A. Don't Wait Up (selected)

Jonah has been late, short, and careful with his phone. Mina checks it because
the dentist story did not survive a second telling.

**Apparent betrayal.** There is a woman named Mara. There is a hotel in another
city. There are two glasses in a photo. Dex is covering for him.

**Innocent / differently guilty reading.** Mara is a recruiter. The hotel is an
interview trip Jonah was ashamed to name. He is not sleeping with her. He is
considering a job in Harboridge and has not asked Mina if she would come.

**Evidence that supports both.** Mara's texts are professionally warm and
occasionally too warm. Maps puts him at The Wren overnight. Instagram looks
like a date. Notes hold a compensation draft and an unsigned offer. Dex's
cover is real either way.

**Final emotional choice.** Is he leaving her, or only failing to tell her he
might have to leave the city? The offer deadline is still open.

**Why it is not Five Minutes.** The other woman is not the destination. Location
does not prove an affair; it proves a trip. The wound is shame and a possible
move, not sex.

### B. The Other Lease

Jonah has been hiding money. A second address appears in Maps. A woman named
Junie is on a lease in Notes. "I love you, I got you" sits in Messages.

**Apparent betrayal.** An apartment for the other woman.

**Innocent reading.** Junie is his sister, out of a marriage, too proud to be
housed in public. The money was rent. The "I love you" is sibling.

**Why it lost.** The sister reveal is easy to write as a twist. Fair seeding
(last name, mom texts, old photos) still leaves the player feeling tricked
rather than wrong. It also sits closer to "another woman + a location"
than it first appears.

### C. Nights with Nora

Jonah is out late. Tender daily texts with Nora. Maps shows a clinic and a
house that is not theirs.

**Apparent betrayal.** A caregiver, or a second life.

**Innocent reading.** His mother is declining. Nora is the aide, or Nora is
the name his mother uses. He hid it because Mina and his mother do not get
along, or because his mother asked him not to say yet.

**Why it lost.** Illness-as-secret leans on pity. A player who misses the
medical register feels cruel rather than mistaken, which is a different
hurt than the one this game is for.

## Why A won

Ordinary. A reasonable player can call it an affair and be wrong without
the case having lied. Three or more apps carry essential evidence. The
ending recasts the hotel, the dinner, and Dex's cover. It is not
"another woman plus a pin on a map proves sex."
